How to spot
fake news

01

Check the source domain

New domains (under 6 months old), misspelled brand names (BBCnews.co vs BBC.com), and sites without editorial policies are major red flags. Always verify the publisher's history.

02

Spot emotional language

Fake news uses fear, outrage, and shock to bypass critical thinking. Words like "BREAKING", "SHOCKING", "They don't want you to know" are classic manipulation tactics.

03

Look for missing citations

Credible articles cite specific studies, institutions, and named experts. Vague phrases like "scientists say" or "experts believe" without links are warning signs.

04

Reverse image search

Photos in fake news are often recycled from unrelated events. Drag images into Google Images or TinEye to find their original context.

05

Cross-check with trusted sources

If a major story is real, Reuters, AP News, BBC, and The Hindu will all cover it. If only one obscure site has the story, treat it with skepticism.

06

Check the date

Old articles are frequently reshared as if they're current news. Always look at the publication date before reacting or sharing.

About TruthLens

Built to fight the
infodemic

TruthLens uses Claude AI to analyze news content across multiple dimensions — from source credibility and citation quality to emotional manipulation and claim verification — to help users make informed decisions about what they read and share.

🧠 How the AI works

We use Claude (claude-sonnet-4-20250514) to perform multi-dimensional analysis. The model evaluates factual accuracy, identifies manipulative language patterns, assesses source reliability signals, and cross-references claims against known fact-checking patterns — all in a single structured prompt with JSON output.

🎯 What we detect

Completely fabricated stories, misleading statistics, out-of-context information, emotional manipulation tactics, unreliable or anonymous sources, sensationalized language, and logical fallacies used to push false narratives.

⚠️ Limitations

AI analysis is a tool to assist judgment, not replace it. Results should be treated as informed guidance, not absolute verdicts. Complex satire, hyper-local events, and very recent news may reduce accuracy. Always cross-check critical claims.

🔒 Privacy

Text you submit is analyzed by the Anthropic API and not stored permanently by TruthLens. No account required. Analysis history is stored only in your browser's local storage and can be cleared at any time.
